Nine Practical Nail-Care Tips Between Appointments

Simple habits can protect your manicure and help you notice problems that need professional care.

Reviewed for Lumi Nails • Updated August 17, 2026

1. Do not use nails as tools

Opening cans, scraping labels and prying objects can chip product or stress the natural nail. Reach for an actual tool instead.

2. Wear gloves for wet work

Long water exposure can contribute to splitting and peeling. Wear gloves for dishwashing and cleaning, and dry hands afterward.

3. Moisturize nails and surrounding skin

Hand cream, cuticle oil or a simple moisturizer can reduce dryness around the nail. Apply after frequent handwashing and polish removal.

4. Never pick or peel product

Peeling gel, dip or enhancements can remove surface layers of the natural nail. Book appropriate removal instead.

5. File snags gently

Smooth a snag before it catches and tears. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends filing in one direction rather than sawing back and forth.

6. Keep toenails comfortably trimmed

Trim toenails straight across to reduce the chance of an ingrown edge. Avoid digging into painful corners.

7. Choose the correct maintenance appointment

Book a refill, removal or new set based on what is currently on your nails. Accurate booking allows enough time and protects the schedule.

8. Speak up about reactions

Stop using a product and seek appropriate medical advice if you develop persistent itching, swelling, blistering or nail separation. A salon cannot diagnose or treat an allergy or infection.

9. Know when to see a dermatologist

Pain, swelling, drainage, a dark streak, persistent discoloration or a nail that changes unexpectedly should be evaluated by a qualified healthcare professional.

Good to know: This guide provides general salon education, not medical advice. Nail condition and product wear vary by person.
Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Should I cut my cuticles at home?

The American Academy of Dermatology advises leaving cuticles alone because they help protect the nail root. Moisturize rough skin instead.

What should I do if gel starts lifting?

Do not peel it. Contact the salon for guidance and keep the area clean and dry.

Can nail products cause an allergic reaction?

Yes, some people react to ingredients in nail products. Persistent itching, swelling, blistering or separation needs medical evaluation.
