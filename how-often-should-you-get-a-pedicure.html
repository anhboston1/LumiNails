How Often Should You Get a Pedicure?

Many guests use a four-to-six-week rhythm, but there is no universal schedule.

Reviewed for Lumi Nails • Updated August 17, 2026

A practical starting point

Many people schedule a pedicure about every four to six weeks for cosmetic maintenance, but there is no medically required salon schedule. Nail growth, polish type, footwear, activity and personal preference all matter.

When you may book sooner

You may prefer a shorter interval when polish appearance is important, your nails grow quickly, you are preparing for travel or an event, or you want regular relaxation. Do not schedule salon care to cover pain, swelling or suspected infection.

When a longer interval may work

A longer interval may be reasonable when you wear no polish, your nails grow slowly and you maintain comfortable length at home. Toenails generally grow more slowly than fingernails.

Know when salon care is not the answer

Painful ingrown nails, redness, swelling, drainage, unexplained discoloration or persistent changes deserve medical attention. People with diabetes, poor circulation or reduced sensation should ask a healthcare professional about safe foot-care routines.

Good to know: This guide provides general salon education, not medical advice. Nail condition and product wear vary by person.
Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is every four weeks too often for a pedicure?

Not necessarily for cosmetic maintenance, provided your nails and skin tolerate the service. Individual needs vary.

Should I get a pedicure before a vacation?

Many guests book several days before travel so polish is fresh and there is time to address a workmanship-related concern.

Can a nail salon treat an ingrown toenail?

A salon provides cosmetic services, not medical treatment. Painful or infected ingrown nails should be evaluated by a qualified healthcare professional.
